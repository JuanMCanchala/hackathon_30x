# 01 · The problem: nobody can prove a service was delivered

## The control that exists for goods

Accounts payable already has a control for goods: the **three-way match**. Before paying, AP
compares the purchase order (what was ordered), the goods-received note (what arrived) and the
invoice (what is charged). If the three agree, the bill can be paid without anyone's judgment.

## The control that does not exist for services

For services there is no goods-received note. Nobody in finance can tell whether the contractor
really worked 160 hours, whether the agency delivered the brand kit, or whether the milestone is
done. So every services bill takes the same path:

1. The bill arrives as a PDF, an e-mail or a WhatsApp message, in Spanish or Portuguese, in COP,
   MXN or BRL.
2. Finance asks the project lead on Slack whether the work was done.
3. Someone waits, then approves on trust.
4. If the bill was wrong (hours not worked, a rate above the contract, a milestone not accepted),
   nobody notices: the check never happened.

## Why it matters for Blinka

Blinka gives US-registered companies with teams in Latin America their business account, local
payouts over **Bre-B, SPEI and Pix**, and yield on idle cash. Its customers pay developers,
designers, agencies, translators and maintenance companies across several countries and
currencies. For them, services are the bulk of what they pay, which is exactly where an agent
that "pays bills" stalls today:

- An agent that pays services bills without evidence is paying on trust, at machine speed.
- An agent that sends every services bill to a person saves nobody any time.
- Most of what goes to a person is a **data problem the vendor could fix** in a minute (a missing
  period, a total that does not add up, hours not yet synced), but nobody asks the vendor first.
- Paying early, or over an international wire, loses yield and fees on every payment.

## What a solution must do

- Prove delivery from **digital evidence** before paying, line by line.
- Pay the part that is proven, and ask for the rest.
- Ask the vendor before bothering the team; reach a person only for what the vendor cannot fix.
- Never pay outside the rules: one improper payment disqualifies.
- Explain every decision so anyone can read why a bill was paid, held or escalated.
- Reconcile every payment against the bank.
